# Frequently Asked Questions

## Q1. What is the difference between Pilates and Yoga?

Pilates focuses on core strength, posture correction, controlled movement, and body alignment.

Yoga often focuses on flexibility, breathing, balance, meditation, and mind-body relaxation.

Both Pilates and Yoga can improve flexibility and body awareness, but Pilates is usually more exercise-based and often uses specialized equipment.

## Q2. Should beginners start with Mat Pilates or Equipment Pilates?

Beginners can start with either Mat Pilates or Equipment Pilates.

Mat Pilates is accessible and helps members learn basic movement control using body weight.

Equipment Pilates can be helpful for beginners because the equipment provides support, resistance, and guidance for correct posture.

Members with pain, posture concerns, or rehabilitation needs may benefit from starting with private equipment Pilates lessons.

## Q3. My body is stiff. Can I still do Pilates?

Yes. Pilates is suitable for people with stiff bodies.

Pilates exercises can be adjusted based on flexibility, strength, and current physical condition.

Beginners do not need to be flexible before starting Pilates. Regular practice may gradually improve mobility, posture, and body control.

## Q4. Is Pilates effective for weight loss?

Pilates can support weight loss by improving muscle tone, posture, body awareness, and exercise consistency.

Pilates alone may not produce rapid weight loss. Results are usually better when Pilates is combined with regular activity, balanced nutrition, and healthy lifestyle habits.

Pilates is especially useful for improving body shape, core strength, and movement quality.

## Q5. How often should I attend classes?

For general fitness, attending Pilates classes two to three times per week is commonly recommended.

Beginners may start with one to two sessions per week and increase frequency as their body adapts.

Consistency is more important than attending too many sessions at once.

## Q6. Can men do Pilates?

Yes. Men can do Pilates.

Pilates is suitable for all genders and can help improve core strength, flexibility, posture, balance, and injury prevention.

Men who do weight training, golf, running, or desk work may benefit from Pilates because it improves body alignment and movement control.

## Q7. I struggle when instructors say “engage your core.” What does it mean?

Engaging your core means activating the deep muscles around your abdomen, pelvis, and lower back to support stable movement.

It should feel controlled and steady, not like holding your breath or forcefully tightening your whole body.

### Breathing Technique

Breathe naturally while keeping your torso stable.

Avoid holding your breath. Try to exhale gently as you prepare for movement or perform the harder part of an exercise.

### Pulling the Navel Inward

Imagine gently pulling your navel inward toward your spine.

The movement should be light and controlled. Avoid sucking in the stomach too strongly or creating unnecessary tension in the shoulders and neck.

### Pelvic and Tailbone Movement

Keep the pelvis stable and avoid excessive arching or rounding of the lower back.

Think of lengthening the tailbone slightly while maintaining comfortable alignment.

If the movement feels painful or confusing, ask the instructor for individual guidance.
